The Technical Fixer 7 SOPs to Build Your Servicing Desk

SOP 1: Morning download and exception review
Goal: 
Catch carrier data differences early and send exceptions to the right reviewer.
Open the agreed download queue in Applied Epic, AMS360, HawkSoft, or EZLynx.
Review new policy change downloads and match each item to the correct client and policy.
Copare key fields against the carrier portal data and existing AMS record.
Flag missing documents, mismatched dates, and data that needs licensed review.
Log each exception with its owner and next step.
Send the queue summary to the service lead; leave commission suspensions and split checks to authorized agency staff.

SOP 2: Service request routing and turnaround tracker
Goal: Give each request a clear owner, expected response, and escalation path.
Log the request, client, policy, arrival time, and requested outcome.
Route routine approved document requests to the VA; send coverage questions to licensed staff.
Set agency-approved targets, such as same-day handling for complete, straightforward document requests.
Route billing concerns to the designated account manager or carrier contact.
Escalate cancellation warnings, potential errors, urgent claims reports, and unclear requests immediately.
Review open items at midday and before close.

SOP 3: COI issuance and delivery proof
Goal: 
Prepare accurate certificates from approved information and document delivery.
Confirm the requester and locate the correct client record and policy.
Compare requested details with agency records and approved certificate procedures.
Prepare the certificate only on an agency-approved template.
Send exceptions, such as requested wording that changes or expands terms, to licensed staff.
Deliver only after required agency approval.
Save the final certificate and record recipient, time, and delivery method.

SOP 4: FNOL intake log
Goal: 
Capture reported facts and route a claim notice without interpreting coverage.
Record the caller's name, contact details, policy reference, and reported loss date.
Note the location, event description, and immediate concerns in the caller's words.
Do not promise payment, assess liability, or state whether coverage applies.
Follow the agency's urgent escalation procedure for injury, severe damage, or immediate danger.
Route the report to the designated licensed staff member and carrier channel.
Record the handoff and follow up on receipt confirmation.

SOP 5: Policy change preparation with a licensed review gate
Goal: 
Make change requests complete before a licensed employee reviews them.
Log the requested change and date received.
Gather required forms, details, and supporting documents.
Check the client and policy references for consistency.
Place the prepared request in the licensed review queue.
Do not bind, alter limits, or submit a binding-level change without the agency's authorized review and approval.
Record the reviewer, decision, and follow-up owner.

SOP 6: Producer protection protocol
Goal: 
Keep producers focused while ensuring important client needs reach the right person.
Define which requests the servicing desk can resolve using written instructions.
Route prospect and sales opportunities to the assigned producer promptly.
Direct routine billing, ID card, and document requests to the service queue.
Interrupt a producer only under agency-defined urgent triggers.
Send coverage questions, potential errors, and uncertain situations to licensed staff.
Review misrouted requests weekly and update the routing rules.

SOP 7: End-of-day service log and E&O documentation habit
Goal: 
Close the day with a clear record of work, open issues, and handoffs.
List completed requests and their final file locations.
Summarize open items, deadlines, and assigned owners.
Confirm that certificates and other approved documents have delivery records.
Note unanswered carrier or client follow-ups.
Record facts and handoffs neutrally; never decide whether an incident is E&O-reportable.
Send the log to the service lead, who assigns any licensed or management review.

Keep all authority boundaries explicit: VAs do not give coverage advice, bind coverage, alter limits, disburse trust funds, or decide whether an incident is E&O-reportable. Licensed agency staff make those decisions.
